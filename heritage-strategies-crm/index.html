CRM AI Employee

Hank

Hank is Heritage's CRM and the foundation for scaling sales. Every prospect lives in one pipeline, tagged by acquisition channel, with last activity, stage, and the next step in view. The same record then carries each client across the full lifecycle, from won through onboarding, active engagement, and renewal. Hank and Foreman are two surfaces on the same platform: clients, tasks, meetings, and documents are modeled once in a shared data layer and used by both.

01 / Overview

The brief

The Heritage AI SaaS model needs a repeatable, scalable sales motion, and a CRM is the foundation it runs on. Hank captures every prospect from first touch, tagged by acquisition channel and owner, then moves it through one pipeline from new lead to won, with last activity, stage, and next step always in view and follow-up drafts ready when a deal goes quiet. The same record then carries each client through onboarding, renewal, and expansion, so Hank covers the full lifecycle, not only the win, with one record per client holding every document, email, call, meeting, and task.

Hank and Foreman share one task and data layer: Foreman runs internal coordination, Hank owns client-level context, and the team toggles between them with one switch. Heritage owns the pipeline and approves every outbound message before it leaves the platform.

02 / Objectives

What success looks like

  • One pipeline for every prospect and client, from new lead to won, tagged by acquisition channel and owner.
  • One client record per prospect and client. Documents (read from SharePoint), emails, calls, meetings, and tasks all surface in one place.
  • A clear stage, last activity date, and next step per record, with a green-amber-red health score visible at a glance.
  • A happy-path checklist tied to each stage. Hank tells the team what should happen next without anyone asking, and flags deals going quiet with a follow-up draft ready.
  • Opportunities carry expected revenue and a next step. Outbound, response, and follow up drafts generated by Hank in Heritage voice, approved by a human before sending. No autonomous outreach.
  • The same record follows each client through onboarding, renewal, and expansion, so Hank covers the full lifecycle, not only the win.
  • Direct collaboration with Foreman: shared task layer, shared data layer, one switch between the two.

03 / Scope

Scope of work

Stage 1 · Months 1 to 2

One pipeline and one client record

Every prospect is captured the moment it arrives, tagged by acquisition channel and owner, and lands in one pipeline from new lead to won. Every document, email, call, meeting, and task attaches to its record.

  • One pipeline for every prospect and client, from new lead to won. Each record is tagged by acquisition channel (referral, website, partner firm, event, other) and owner.
  • One record per prospect and client. Holds all linked documents, emails, calls, meetings, and tasks.
  • Inbound capture from Heritage Strategies and Heritage AI website forms creates a Prospect record with source and owner.
  • Automatic email logging through Outlook integration. Emails to or from a tracked stakeholder land on the client record.
  • Documents surface on the client record via SharePoint integration: scopes of work, meeting letters, supporting files. Documents stay in SharePoint; Hank reads them where they live.
  • Stakeholder map per client (principals, spouse, attorneys, accountants, partner advisors) populated from logged emails and meeting letters.
  • Customer type tag per record (e.g. HNW family, partner-firm client, board, other) for filtered views and routing.
  • Conversational lookup by chat or voice for client-level questions with sourced answers.

Stage 2 · Months 3 to 4

Pipeline stages, follow-up cadence, and opportunities

Each client moves through pipeline stages with a happy-path checklist Hank advances. Hank flags deals going quiet and drafts the follow-up, and a green, amber, red health score gives the team an at-a-glance read.

  • Each client carries a pipeline stage. Heritage defines the stages (e.g. New Lead, Engaged, Qualified, Active Client, Churned). Hank tracks where each client sits and the next step.
  • Follow-up cadence: Hank flags any deal or client going quiet against its stage and drafts the next outreach so nothing slips.
  • Happy-path checklist per stage. Tasks auto-instantiate when a client enters a stage and land in the shared Foreman task layer with named owner and due date.
  • Account health per client (On Track, At Risk, Critical) with a plain-language reason: days in stage, overdue items, meeting cadence.
  • Morning client digest: who moved stages, who is at risk, what to look at today.
  • Opportunity object on the client record: name, type (insurance, investment, expansion, other), expected revenue, status, owner, due date. Hank surfaces open opportunities in the morning client digest.
  • Pre-meeting prep summary delivered before the meeting: last interactions, open obligations, current scope of work, recent vault changes.

Stage 3 · Months 5 to 6

Outbound drafts, account management, and connectors

Outbound, response, and follow up drafts generated by Hank when something needs to go out. The same record carries each client through onboarding, renewal, and expansion. Heritage AI app linkage and the Foreman environment switcher live.

  • Outbound, response, and follow up drafts generated by Hank in Heritage voice when something needs to go out. Heritage employees approve before sending.
  • Account management across the lifecycle: the won record continues through onboarding, active engagement, renewal, and expansion, with stage-based tasks at each step.
  • Heritage AI app linkage: book production status visible on the client record. Document inventory linked to tasks.
  • Environment switcher between Hank and Foreman (and Celeste when live). Single sign-on. Shared data layer for Tasks, Meetings, Documents, and Stakeholders modeled once.
  • Additional connectors available after alignment with Heritage.

06 / Required inputs

What Heritage provides

Inputs are grouped by stage. Stage 1 inputs are required for launch. Stage 2 and Stage 3 inputs are required by the start of those stages. Approval delays extend cadence day for day. Supernal inventories what is reusable upfront and only asks Heritage to fill the gaps.

6.1   Engagement and approvals

  • Single named approver for client stages, happy paths, and outbound voice.
  • Weekly 30-minute review call during build.

6.2   Stage 1 inputs

  • Active prospect and client list with current owner and last interaction.
  • Stakeholder taxonomy (examples: principal, spouse, attorney, accountant, partner advisor, other).
  • Heritage Strategies and Heritage AI website form endpoints.
  • Outlook and email read access for auto-logging. Microsoft 365 single sign-on.
  • SharePoint read access to client folders so Hank can surface scopes of work, meeting letters, and supporting files on each client record.

6.3   Stage 2 inputs

  • Heritage's client stages with entry and exit criteria per stage.
  • Happy-path checklist per stage: the tasks the team typically does on a client at that stage.
  • Definition of On Track, At Risk, and Critical for the health score (signals and thresholds).

6.4   Stage 3 inputs

  • 5 to 10 examples of existing Heritage outbound copy.
  • Style guide: tone, do and don't, banned phrases. Locked before drafts go live.
  • Heritage AI app read-only access for book status and document inventory.
