Project Manager AI Employee · Heritage Strategies

Foreman

Heritage's shared workspace for everything in flight. Every client engagement, every internal project, every meeting, and every action item lives in one place. One owner, one due date, one status per task, in line with the Heritage no-dates standard.

01 / Overview

The brief

Foreman replaces today's mix of Excel meeting trackers, SharePoint scope documents, and Outlook updates. It captures meetings, drafts post-meeting summaries, chases delegated items, and answers questions like "where are we on the Karol case" on demand, by chat or by voice.

Foreman also absorbs the executive-assistant responsibilities that have direct leverage on engagement delivery: calendar awareness, delegated-item follow-up, and Tom Sr.'s daily digest. Foreman is delivered in three phases. Each phase ships work the team uses from day one.

02 / Objectives

What success looks like

  • End dual entry across Excel, SharePoint, Outlook, and Word. One shared list of work, kept current.
  • Make Heritage's prep protocol stick. Every engagement runs through Pre-Internal, IR1, IR2, and any IR3+ steps on schedule, with materials ready before the meeting.
  • Give Tom Sr. and the team one trustworthy way to ask about client status by chat or by voice. Sourced answers in seconds, from anywhere.
  • Eliminate manual follow-up. Foreman tracks every open task and sends reminders on its own.
  • Replace ad-hoc check-ins with a morning digest of priorities and an end-of-day digest of overdue items.
  • Surface calendar conflicts, prep gaps, and delegated-item follow-ups directly to Operations so manual chasing disappears.
  • Enforce the Heritage standard on every action item: owner, due date, next step. No item leaves a meeting without all three.

03 / Scope

Scope of work

Phase 1 · Months 1–2

Workspace, meeting capture, and daily digests

Foreman sits in on every Monday meeting, turns it into a structured record the team can act on, and gives the team one trustworthy way to ask about client status by chat or voice.

  • Records and transcribes meetings.
  • For client engagements with a defined cadence, Foreman tracks each prep step (Pre-Internal, IR1, IR2, Partner Review) against the schedule and flags any delays before the next meeting.
  • After each meeting, Foreman drafts a structured post-meeting summary letter from the transcript. Once a Point Person signs off, that letter becomes the source of truth for what was agreed.
  • Action items, owners, and due dates are extracted from the transcript and proposed as tasks. Duplicates are merged automatically.
  • Tasks missing an owner or due date wait in a review queue. They go live only after a Point Person fills in both fields. This enforces the Heritage no-dates standard at the source.
  • Audit trail: every transcript, draft action item, and human approval is timestamped and searchable.
  • A meeting list view shows every upcoming and past meeting at a glance, with type and owner.
  • Schedule changes are flagged and subject to approval.
  • Shared workspace, basic view: a single workspace for Heritage's active client engagements and internal projects. Each project shows current phase, scheduled meetings, open action items, owners, due dates, and source documents.
  • One-time migration from today's Excel meeting trackers and SharePoint scopes. After that, updates flow in from Outlook, recorded meetings, and direct edits in the app.
  • Web and mobile interface.
  • Read-only Ask Anything: any team member can ask Foreman a question in plain English by chat or by voice on a phone. Answers come back in seconds, with sources.
  • Every Foreman answer links back to its source (meeting letter, task, or calendar event). Verifiable in one click.
  • Each project or engagement carries a status (On Track, At Risk, Critical) with a plain-language reason. The Monday rundown is built from these statuses.
  • A color-coded view (green, amber, red) makes the Monday rundown readable at a glance.
  • Morning digest: today's meetings, prep gaps, due items, top three risks.
  • End-of-day digest: what was completed, what is overdue, what needs a new commitment.
  • A separate summary covers top risks, schedule changes, and items needing escalation.
  • Completion is set in-app or detected from meeting letters. Overdue or ambiguous items go to the Operations review queue for confirmation. Email-signal completion arrives via the Phase 3 connector.
  • Calendar conflicts, prep gaps, and delegated-item follow-ups surfaced directly to Operations so manual chasing disappears.

Phase 2 · Months 3–4

Full task management and voice-to-task

Foreman runs the team's task list with the depth and views Heritage needs to manage every commitment from a single hub. Tom Sr.'s voice notes turn into assigned tasks on the right person's list.

  • Tasks have an owner, a due date, a status (To Do, In Progress, Blocked, Done), a priority (Low, Normal, High, Urgent), and labels for filtering.
  • Sub-tasks split work into smaller pieces. Dependencies show which task blocks which.
  • Recurring tasks and templates: routine work (weekly check-ins, monthly reports) and repeating projects (partner onboarding) load with no manual setup.
  • Multiple views of the same tasks: list, board (Kanban), calendar, and timeline (Gantt). Each person works in the view that fits.
  • Comments and @mentions on every task. File attachments per task. Activity feed shows who did what and when, and serves as the audit trail.
  • Watchers stay informed without being assigned. Notifications via in-app, email, or MS Teams.
  • Auto-pings on delegated items past their reply window so manual chasing stays gone.
  • Monday rundown shown live with On Track, At Risk, Critical colors.
  • Tom Sr. records a voice note and Foreman creates or updates the corresponding tasks. Senior issues direction. Foreman puts it on the right person's list.
  • Voice-to-task respects existing engagement structure: tasks land in the right project with the right phase tagged and the right Point Person assigned.
  • Foreman confirms back to Tom Sr. by chat or voice with a list of what was created or updated, with sources.

Phase 3 · Months 5–6 · menu-priced

Connectors

Foreman pulls signals from email, calls, Salesforce, and the client vault into one operating picture. Each connector is quoted separately.

  • Salesforce sync: read and write of records, opportunities, and notes against the existing Heritage Salesforce instance.
  • Heritage AI client-vault linkage: Foreman reads the document checklist per client and surfaces gaps to the responsible Point Person.
  • Email scanning for client commitments: completion signals from email activity feed back into Foreman accountability layer.
  • Auto-drafted post-meeting letters into SharePoint, in Heritage voice, ready for Point Person sign-off.
  • Phone-call ingestion: Foreman captures inbound and outbound calls and turns key commitments into tasks.

06 / Required inputs

What Heritage provides in week 0–2

For Foreman to be operational on day one, Heritage provides the following inputs in week 0–2. Each day of approval delay extends the timeline by one day. Several items may already exist within Supernal's stack from prior Heritage work. We will inventory what is reusable in week 0 and only ask Heritage to provide the gaps.

6.1   Engagement and approvals

  • Single named approver to sign off on the workspace structure, phase templates, and prep-protocol rules.
  • Weekly 30-minute review call during build. Operations (Book Production) is the named product owner and primary feedback channel.

6.2   Task and project definitions

  • Standard project types and the typical task list for each.
  • Default task statuses (To Do, In Progress, Blocked, Done) and priorities (Low, Normal, High, Urgent), or Heritage-preferred names for each.
  • Recurring tasks (weekly, monthly, quarterly) and their owners.
  • Role definitions and ownership boundaries.
  • Existing meeting-summary or post-meeting letter format so action items are extracted the way the team writes them today. Notification preferences per role.
  • Existing IRR1 and IRR2 protocol definitions, plus any additional follow-up cadences in current use.

6.3   Client and engagement data

  • Current Excel meeting trackers, SharePoint scopes, and active meeting letters (one-time export for migration).
  • Client list with current phase, owner, and book-production status.
  • Internal project list.

6.4   Operational access

  • Outlook calendar and SharePoint access.
  • MS Teams for digest delivery.
  • Heritage AI platform API access.

07 / Acceptance

Phase 1 acceptance

Phase 1 is accepted when, for two consecutive weeks, the following are true:

  • Every Monday-meeting action item leaves with an owner and a due date. No item bypasses the review queue.
  • The morning and end-of-day digests deliver on time to every named recipient.
  • Tom Sr. asks Foreman at least three client-status questions by voice or chat in the week.
  • Operations confirms a measurable reduction in manual follow-up volume against a baseline captured in week 0.
  • No P1 incidents open at sign-off.

Acceptance is signed by Operations (Book Production) and countersigned by the Partner.
